Everything flows one way: sensors, then "where am I", then drivers, the mux, ego, and one backend. The layers only talk through ROS topics, so any box can be swapped without touching the others.

The picture

1. Sensors: raw data

Sensor Driver Publishes
Wheel speed an Arduino on the Orin's CAN bus (0x200, 0x201), read by spine_bridge role:=sensors (Today's wiring) /vehicle/odom, /vehicle/wheels
IMU (Yahboom, USB) aav_imu_yahboom (Rust), then the stock imu_filter_madgwick /sensors/imu/data_raw, then /sensors/imu/data
GPS (u-blox, USB) the stock ublox_gps; or nmea_navsat_driver, or ublox_dgnss on the car /sensors/gps/fix
The car's shape the stock robot_state_publisher, from vehicle.yaml /tf_static: where each sensor sits
Lidar (Velodyne VLP-16, roof) the stock velodyne driver, on the Orin /sensors/lidar/points
Front cameras (the camera Pi) MediaMTX on the Pi streams H.264; on the Orin one camera_stream_<camera> each decodes it and runs the detector inside (camera pipeline) /perception/detections: no raw pictures on the network

Each driver publishes a standard message in its own frame. Another IMU or GPS can replace it without changing anything downstream.

2. Where am I: two filters

  • ekf_local fuses the wheels and the IMU. It's smooth and never jumps, but it drifts slowly, like counting your steps. Use it to steer.
  • ekf_global fuses the same inputs plus GPS. It stays near the truth, but can hop a little when a GPS fix arrives. Use it to plan.
  • navsat_transform turns GPS latitude and longitude into map x and y for ekf_global.

Does the global EKF read the local one?

In effect, yes. ekf_global publishes only the correction map → odom, worked out on top of ekf_local's odom → base_link. Chained together, they give the car's true position, and the local estimate stays smooth.

3. Drivers propose, the mux picks

Every driver publishes a TwistStamped (speed in m/s, turn rate in rad/s) on its own topic. None of them knows what kind of vehicle it's driving.

Driver Topic Priority
Joystick: the safety driver /drivers/joy/cmd_vel 100
RC transmitter, on "RC via ROS" (proposed, TODO 48) /drivers/rc/cmd_vel 95
Keyboard, or another remote /drivers/keyboard/cmd_vel 90
Actuator tests: measure the car (stages 3-4) /drivers/tests/cmd_vel 60
Scripted: speed up, hold, slow down /drivers/scripted/cmd_vel 50
Replay of a recorded drive /drivers/replay/cmd_vel 40
Drive to point: drive to one GPS point and stop /drivers/point/cmd_vel 25
Nav2: drive to a point on the map /drivers/nav/cmd_vel 20
AI /drivers/ai/cmd_vel 10, later

twist_mux passes on the highest priority that has spoken in the last 0.5 s, as /control/cmd_vel. Publishing true on /safety/estop blocks every driver until someone publishes false.

Teleop is just another driver. The keyboard's commands take the same path as Nav2's: mux, ego, then the turtle or the car.

4. ego: the virtual car

ego is the only node that knows the car is a car. Fifty times a second it turns /control/cmd_vel into /ego/actuator_cmd: an AckermannDriveStamped with a speed and a steering angle (positive is left).

Step What it does
Bicycle model steering angle = atan(wheelbase × turn rate ÷ speed). A car can't turn on the spot, so at zero speed ego holds the wheels where they are.
Limits 1.0 m/s top speed to start; acceleration; steering ±0.5 rad; steering rate
Timeout no command for 0.3 s: brake to a stop

5. One car model, everywhere

The car's numbers live in one file, ros/bringup/aav_description/config/vehicle.yaml. The launch files hand them to every part that needs them.

Part Uses
ego the wheelbase and steering limit, for the bicycle model
URDF (robot_state_publisher) where each sensor sits
Nav2 the footprint, and the tightest turn: wheelbase ÷ tan(max steer) = 2.87 m
turtlesim_adapter, gazebo_adapter the wheelbase, so the turtle and the simulated car turn like the car
aav_spine, aav_legacy the wheelbase, track and wheel size

Most of the numbers are still guesses: see Car numbers. The same numbers make the car's linear model (transfer functions, state space, the margins with the delays): The car as a linear model.

6. Backends: one plays the car

Backend Package Does
The car, today (actuators:=uart) aav_legacy legacy_uart_bridge on the Motor Pi: UART frames for the ESP32
The CAN spine, later (actuators:=can) aav_spine_bridge spine_bridge on the Orin: CAN frames for the spine firmware
turtlesim aav_turtlesim the stock turtlesim, steered with the bicycle model
Gazebo aav_gazebo a simulated car with an IMU and GPS, on the real car's topics
CARLA aav_carla adapter ready, untested: needs the NVIDIA computer (CARLA)

Each backend reports the car's speed back on /vehicle/odom. That closes the loop into the EKFs. On today's car the UART link sends nothing back, so the wheel sensors on the Orin's CAN bus do it.

The output mux: one target, and the real car only on real data

No backend reads /ego/actuator_cmd itself. The output mux (target:=) passes ego's command on to its target's own topic, and the sensor mux (source:=) checks where the data comes from. See Sources and targets.

ego ─/ego/actuator_cmd─► output_mux ─/control/car/actuator_cmd─► legacy_uart_bridge (today),
                           ▲                                      or spine_bridge role spine
                           │  (only while every source is REAL; else silent, latched)
        sensor_mux ────────┘  /system/sensor_mux/state

7. Safety layers

From the person outward. Each layer works even if everything after it fails.

# Layer Stops the car when
1 Joystick deadman (priority 100) held with the sticks centred
2 /safety/estop it's true: the mux blocks every driver, and the car backends brake
3 ego's timeout no command arrives for 0.3 s
3a The output mux any source isn't REAL (a simulator, the bench, a replay on the network), or the sensor mux says no or goes quiet: it sends nothing, latched
4 The backend ego is quiet: legacy_uart_bridge sends brake frames after 0.3 s; spine_bridge (CAN spine) stops sending drive frames after 0.1 s
5 The firmware's watchdog no valid frame for 100 ms: throttle off, brake on. The Rust ESP32 firmware, on the UART link today, or the CAN spine
6 RC override switch, hardware e-stop, driver faults always: handled in the firmware

Two more, for the automatic drivers only (Nav2 and the AI slot; never a person or a test):

Layer Stops Nav2 when
collision_monitor radar or lidar points in the stop zone just ahead; or the radar or lidar goes quiet for 1 s
The sensor watchdog (aav_safety) the wheels, the IMU, the GPS or an EKF go quiet, send NaN or freeze; the GPS jumps; the wheels disagree with the IMU or the GPS; the HUD's screen freezes or dies (its heartbeat, on the car); a front camera's stream goes quiet (its detections, on the car); the watchdog itself goes quiet (fail-safe: twist_mux's lock times out). A twist_mux lock, shown on the HUD and the map window (simulation)

Today's car has no layer 5

The 2025-26 ESP32 firmware has no timeout. If the bridge dies, the last throttle stays on. Keep the wheels off the ground and the e-stop in hand: see Firmware. So, with frame_version: 1, the bridge locks the programs out: twist_mux's no-timeout lock (60) holds scripted, replay, point, Nav2 and AI. Only people and the actuator tests drive.
