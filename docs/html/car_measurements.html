The car's measurements live in one file, ros/bringup/aav_description/config/vehicle.yaml. Every other part reads them from there, so a number only ever changes in one place.

Who reads it What it takes
urdf/aav.urdf.xacro, then robot_state_publisher, then /tf_static body, wheels, and every sensor's mount (the frames)
aav_ego (ego.launch.py) wheelbase, steering limit, reverse speed; and its body: mass, track, centre of mass, tyre grip, the throttle's and brake's accelerations
aav_spine (spine.launch.py) wheelbase, track
aav_legacy (legacy.launch.py) wheel diameter, encoder counts per revolution
aav_navigation (navigation.launch.py) footprint (both costmaps), minimum turning radius (SmacPlannerHybrid), reverse speed
aav_turtlesim, aav_gazebo wheelbase, and in Gazebo the whole model
aav_drive_to_point, carla_adapter actuators.max_brake; actuators.max_accel
firmware/spine (spine-core) copies of max_steering_angle and max_reverse_speed (it can't read the file). Change both together: test_vehicle.py checks they match

aav_description/aav_description/vehicle.py does the derived maths: turning radius = wheelbase / tan(max steering angle), and the footprint polygon from the body. Its tests (ros/bringup/aav_description/test/test_vehicle.py) run without ROS.

Every value is still a guess

Each TODO in vehicle.yaml came from the previous teams' code, with the file and line cited next to it, or is a guess. Two examples: the wheelbase (1.57 m) comes from a MATLAB script, and the old code's steering limit varies from 0.45 to 0.55 rad. Measure the car, replace the number and delete the TODO.

The frames

REP-103 and REP-105 conventions:

  • metres and radians;
  • x forward, y left, z up;
  • angles positive counter-clockwise, so a positive steering angle turns left.
Frame Where
base_link On the ground, under the centre of the rear axle. Everything is measured from here.
chassis The body box, from body.length/width/height and rear_overhang
imu_link, gps_link, radar_link, camera_left_link, camera_right_link, camera_rear_link, lidar_link sensors.<name>.xyz (m) and rpy (rad), relative to base_link
*_steering_joint, *_wheel_joint Front wheels steer about z; all wheels spin about y

Why it matters:

  • navsat_transform uses base_link -> gps_link to remove the antenna offset from every GPS fix, so measure the antenna itself, not the receiver box. It is mounted over the rear axle (Ben, 23 Oct), which is best: a heading error can't move the GPS position, and reversing keeps its heading (Learn: GPS).
  • The EKFs use imu_link to rotate IMU readings into the car's frame. If the IMU is mounted turned or upside down, its rpy must say so, or yaw comes out backwards.

How to measure

The tape-measure route, step by step, with pictures: the measuring map (pixi run measure-map; --readings turns chained readings into these numbers).

  1. Park on level ground with the wheels straight.
  2. Mark base_link: plumb down from the centre of the rear axle and chalk the spot.
  3. Measure each item with a tape:
    • wheelbase: rear axle centre to front axle centre;
    • track: left wheel centre to right wheel centre;
    • wheel diameter: across the tyre, loaded;
    • body: bumper to bumper, side to side, ground to the top, and the rear overhang behind the rear axle;
    • each sensor: x forward and y left of the chalk mark, z up from the ground.
  4. Steering limit: turn to full lock and measure the angle of the inside and outside front wheels. The value in the file is the "virtual" centre wheel. A good approximation is atan(wheelbase / R), where R is the turning radius to the middle of the rear axle. Measure R by driving a full circle at full lock at walking pace.
  5. Mass and centre of mass: four bathroom scales, then the rear lifted (measure-the-car.md, M7 and M7b). See "Weight and balance" below.

Weight and balance

Ego knows the car's weight (aav_ego/dynamics.py). Most of it is guessed today.

vehicle.yaml Today Measured by
mass 181 kg (Ben: about 400 lb) M7: the four scales added up
centre_of_mass.x 0.70 m ahead of the rear axle (a guess) M7: wheelbase × front ÷ total
centre_of_mass.height 0.55 m (a guess) M7b: the rear lifted, the front weighed again
tyre_grip 0.7 g (dry asphalt: 0.7-0.9) test 4.3: full brake; if the wheels skid, the deceleration in g
actuators.max_accel, max_brake 1.5 and 3.0 m/s², empty tests 4.1 and 4.3; 4.9 and 4.10 again with a person aboard

What ego does with them:

The load (people, cargo) changes The load doesn't change
how hard the car can speed up: the same motor, more mass how fast it may take a turn: sliding and tipping don't depend on the mass
how hard it can brake, so how far it needs to stop
the energy in a crash: ½ m v²
  • Speeding up: ego never asks for more than the motor can give the loaded car: max_accel × mass ÷ (mass + payload).
  • Turns: ego slows the car so the sideways acceleration stays under 2 m/s², or under half of what slides or tips the car if that is lower. It keeps the turn the driver asked for and lowers the speed, so the car stays on its path. At full lock (2.87 m) that is 2.4 m/s: it only matters above the car's 1 m/s, so today in the simulator. Checked in Gazebo (22 Oct, sim_speed:=3): asked 3 m/s at full lock, ego allowed 2.40 m/s and the car held 2.37-2.43 m/s, 1.9-2.0 m/s² sideways.
  • Braking: never softened. If the load means the brakes can't stop the car as fast as ego plans (max_deceleration), ego warns at start-up and when the payload changes.
  • Payload is a parameter (ego.yaml, 0 kg): ros2 param set /ego payload 80 when someone gets in.

Tipping matters on this car: a 77 cm track is narrow for a 1.5 m tall car. The car tips when the sideways acceleration reaches g × (track ÷ 2) ÷ (height of the centre of mass): 6.9 m/s² at 0.55 m high, but 3.8 m/s² at 1 m. Ego keeps to half of that.

From M7 and M7b (any unit, as long as all four are the same):

from aav_description import vehicle
v = vehicle.load()['vehicle']
x, height = vehicle.centre_of_mass(v, front=FL + FR, rear=RL + RR,
                                   front_lifted=FL2 + FR2, lift=0.25)   # lift in m

Look at it

You need Jazzy installed and the workspace built: see Run it.

ros2 launch aav_description view.launch.py

RViz opens with the car model and every frame's axes (red x forward, green y left, blue z up). Each sensor is an orange box. A slider window moves the steering and wheel joints.

The shapes are only for looking at, and every size comes from vehicle.yaml: a red tub over the wheels, a cabin with a white roof and a seat, a bumper and headlights at the front, and a white mark on each wheel so you can see it turn. Physics and Nav2 use only the collision shapes: the body's outline between the bumpers, and the four wheels.

After editing vehicle.yaml, rebuild and relaunch:

colcon build --packages-select aav_description --symlink-install

With --symlink-install, later edits to vehicle.yaml only need a relaunch.

Two other ways to inspect the model:

  • Print the generated URDF: xacro ros/bringup/aav_description/urdf/aav.urdf.xacro.
  • Draw the frame tree as a diagram: ros2 run rqt_tf_tree rqt_tf_tree.

In Gazebo

aav.gazebo.xacro adds the simulation parts when sim:=true:

  • the Ackermann steering system (gz-sim 8);
  • IMU and NavSat sensors;
  • the joint-state publisher.

Gazebo builds the simulated car from the same vehicle.yaml, so the simulated car turns like the real one. Its odometry frames are bridged but its TF is not: robot_localization owns odom -> base_link, exactly as on the car.
