Part A. The problem

1. Bad things that could happen

Bad thing Looks like Usual cause
It sways wider and wider round the path correcting too hard, too late
It cuts a corner over the kerb the plan ignores the car's width
It drives into something a parked car, a person the path was wrong, or old
It reacts too late brakes, still hits it only looked at distance, not speed
It freezes stopped for ever in a clear road a rule with no way out
It has no command the computer has no answer rules that can't all be met
It trusts a bad reading swerves at nothing a position jump, a false detection
It asks the impossible "turn now" at full lock forgetting the machine's limits

Every card below exists to stop one of these.

2. Three promises

Promise Stops which bad things Maths word
Stable swaying Lyapunov stability
Safe driving into things, cutting corners, late reactions forward invariance
Never stuck no command; freezing recursive feasibility; liveness

You have it when you can sort each row of card 1 under one promise.

3. Why testing isn't enough

  • A test shows it worked on the routes we drove.
  • A proof shows it works on every route that fits the assumptions.
  • Neither replaces the other: a proof is only as good as its assumptions, and tests are how you check those.

Watch: "verification vs validation autonomous vehicles". You have it when you can say why 1,000 good runs don't prove the 1,001st.

Part B. Describing a car

4. State

What: the short list of numbers that says everything about the car right now. Why: every proof is a statement about how these numbers move. On our car: how far left of the path, heading error, speed, steering angle, and six more for the lags. Ten numbers. Watch: "state space representation control" (Brian Douglas; Steve Brunton's "Control Bootcamp"). You have it when you can say why position alone isn't a state. (Two cars at the same spot, one stopped and one at 5 m/s, have different futures.)

5. Model

What: the rule for the next state from this one and the command. next state = f(state, command). Why: to plan ahead, or prove anything, you must be able to predict. On our car: the bicycle model: two wheels' worth of geometry stands for four. Watch: "kinematic bicycle model". You have it when you can say what the model leaves out (tyre slip, slopes, wind).

6. Feedback

What: measure, compare with what you want, correct, repeat. Why: it is the loop that can go unstable, and the loop we make promises about. Watch: "open loop vs closed loop control". You have it when you can explain why a thermostat is feedback and a timer isn't.

7. Limits

What: what the machine can't do: steer past full lock, steer faster than its motor, brake harder than its brakes. Why: most proofs in textbooks quietly assume no limits. Ours can't. On our car: full lock 0.5 rad; steering rate 0.6 rad/s. Watch: "actuator saturation", "anti-windup". You have it when you can say what happens to a correction that asks for more than full lock.

8. Delay: dead time and lag

What: dead time = nothing happens at first. Lag = it then catches up along a curve. Why: delay is the usual reason a stable design sways on a real machine. On our car: the steering's dead time and its motor's lag; both still to measure. Watch: "time delay control systems", "dead time first order plus dead time". You have it when you can explain why steering a boat is harder than steering a bike.

Part C. Stable

9. Stability

What: after a push, the error comes back to zero and stays. Why: the first promise. Picture: a marble in a bowl (stable), on a table (neither), on a ball (unstable). Watch: "stability of dynamical systems intuition".

10. Linear systems and poles

What: for simple (linear) models, a few numbers called poles tell you everything: does the error die, ring, or grow. Why: the quickest stability check there is, and how our trackers were tuned. Watch: "poles and stability", "eigenvalues stability" (Steve Brunton). You have it when you know which side of zero a pole must be on.

11. Lyapunov functions

What: one number V that is zero on the path, bigger the worse the error, and goes down every step. If you can find one, the error must shrink. Why: it proves stability without solving the equations, and it works for non-simple models. Barrier functions (card 17) copy this idea. The line: V(next) < V(now) whenever the error isn't zero. Watch: "Lyapunov stability intuition" (MIT "Underactuated Robotics", Russ Tedrake). You have it when you can explain why "energy always falling" means "it stops".

12. Why delay breaks it

What: a correction that arrives late pushes the wrong way. Why: this is the gap between the papers and our car. Watch: "phase margin time delay", "Smith predictor". You have it when you can explain why turning the shower tap too eagerly burns you.

Part D. MPC

13. Optimisation

What: choose the numbers that make a score as small as possible, while obeying rules. Why: MPC is an optimisation solved 20 times a second. Words: cost = the score. Constraint = a rule. Feasible = obeys every rule. Watch: "convex optimization intuition" (Stephen Boyd's lectures for depth).

14. Quadratic programs (QP)

What: the easy kind: a bowl-shaped score, straight-line rules. One best answer, found fast. Why: it is what our solver solves. A rule only helps us if it can be written as a straight line in the plan. Watch: "quadratic programming explained". You have it when you know why a bowl has one lowest point and a crumpled sheet doesn't.

15. MPC

What: each tick: imagine the next 1.5 s, find the best plan that obeys the rules, do the first move, throw the rest away, repeat. Why: it is the one controller that handles limits and looks ahead. Every promise in this survey becomes a rule inside it. Watch: "model predictive control" (MATLAB Tech Talks' "Understanding MPC"). You have it when you can say why it throws away 29 of its 30 planned moves.

16. Horizon and the end of the plan

What: the plan only sees 1.5 s. What happens after is invisible to it. Why: a plan can obey every rule for 1.5 s and end in a hopeless spot. This is where "never stuck" is won or lost (card 23). Watch: "MPC terminal cost terminal constraint". You have it when you can describe a plan that is fine for 1.5 s and doomed at 1.6.

Part E. Safe

17. Safe sets and invariance

What: the safe set is everywhere the car is allowed to be. Invariant means: once inside, always inside. Why: "safe" in maths is exactly "the safe set is invariant". Watch: "invariant sets control", "forward invariance".

18. Barrier functions

What: a number H: positive = safe, zero = the edge, negative = inside. Why: it turns "stay out of that region" into arithmetic. On our car: H = (distance to the object's centre)² − radius². Watch: "control barrier functions" (talks by Aaron Ames).

19. The barrier rule (CBF)

What: H(next) ≥ (1 − γ) × H(now): each tick may use only the share γ of the room left. Why: this is the idea at the centre. Guide steps 3 to 6. The proof: six lines of induction. Guide step 5. You have it when you can do guide step 5 without looking.

20. Two ways to use it

A filter Inside the MPC
How check each command, change it as little as possible add the rule to the plan
Sees ahead? no yes
Can steer round? barely: it mostly slows yes
Size tiny bigger

Watch: "CBF QP safety filter", "MPC with control barrier functions". You have it when you can say why the filter tends to get stuck and the MPC less so.

21. How many steps until the command matters

What: steering changes heading, heading changes position. The command reaches the distance only after two steps. This is called relative degree. Why: a barrier on position can't be held by this tick's steering alone. Either plan ahead (MPC), or build the delay into the barrier. Watch: "relative degree nonlinear control", "high order control barrier functions".

Part F. Never stuck, and the mess

22. Feasibility

What: is there any plan that obeys all the rules? Why: if not, the solver has no answer. Add limits (card 7) and a barrier (card 19), and they can ask opposite things. On our car: "don't get closer" and "can't brake that hard" collided (guide step 7).

23. Recursive feasibility and the trusted fallback

What: if there was an answer this tick, there is one next tick. How it is proved: make every plan end somewhere a simple, trusted controller can take over and stay safe for ever. Then last tick's plan, shifted by one, plus the trusted move, is an answer for this tick. On our car: the trusted controller is "stop". Guide step 8. Watch: "recursive feasibility MPC", "terminal invariant set". You have it when you can explain why "can always stop in time" is a safe set.

24. Stuck in the other sense

What: the solver answers, the car is safe, and it sits there for ever. Why: a barrier only pushes away. It can't choose left or right, or back out. On our car: at 1 m/s, head-on, it stops short and stays. Watch: "deadlock control barrier functions", "liveness".

25. When the assumptions are wrong

Assumption Wrong how Field of study
the model is right tyres, slopes, load robust MPC, tube MPC
disturbances are small wind, bumps input-to-state stability (ISS)
the car knows where it is error, jumps estimation; safe control under uncertainty
we know where things are missed or false detections perception uncertainty
the command acts at once dead time predictor methods, delay-aware CBFs

Why: each row turns "proved" into "proved, if". The research is in shrinking the ifs. Watch: "robust MPC tube", "input to state stability intuition".

Part G. Learning, and what is open

26. Where machine learning fits

ML supplies If it is wrong Does the promise survive?
the goal or the path the car safely follows a poor path yes
where the objects are the car avoids the wrong places no
part of the model predictions are off only with a bound on the error

The pattern: the learned part proposes, the proved part guarantees. Watch: "safe learning in robotics", "safe reinforcement learning shielding".

Three statements, in plain words

1 If the room is never negative at the start, and each tick uses at most a share of it, it is never negative γ between 0 and 1; the rule really holds guide step 5
2 If the car can always stop in the room it has, and asks to stop the moment that is about to become false, it stops in time a known worst-case braking and delay guide step 8
3 If every plan ends where the fallback can take over, there is always a next plan the fallback keeps the car in the safe set card 23
