Ben decided it on 23 Oct ("mediamtx on the pi to the orin seems like the best choice"; decisions). This page is how it's built, the settings and why, what was measured on a Mac, and what to check at the car.

The short version

  • The camera Pi streams H.264 (1280x720, 15 fps, 2 Mbit/s) with MediaMTX.
  • On the Orin, one program per camera (camera_stream) unpacks it, undistorts it and runs RF-DETR Nano. Raw pictures never cross the network.
  • On a Mac, the whole chain takes 27 ms from the picture to its detections: GStreamer with VideoToolbox, now the default. Measured with pixi run stream-check.
  • Calibrating the cameras at the car: pixi run calibrate-camera (camera calibration).

Why the detections, not /diagnostics

The node sends detections for every picture, boxes or none: 15 a second, so 1 s of silence is a sure sign, as for a camera's pictures. /diagnostics comes once a second, and its frame rate is over 5 s. Nothing checks for a frozen picture: an empty list repeats on a clear road, and a stuck Pi camera stops the stream instead.

The settings, and why

Setting Value Why
Codec H.264 about 10 times smaller than JPEG; every decoder has it
Encoder MediaMTX's softwareH264 (OpenH264) the Pi 5 has no H.264 hardware
Size, rate 1280x720, 15 fps enough for the detector (it shrinks to 384 px); half the Pi's work of 1080p
Bitrate 2 Mbit/s (max 2.4) 4 Mbit/s for both: 0.4% of the Orin's cable
Key frame every 15 frames = 1 s a lost or new reader waits at most 1 s for a picture
Profile baseline no B-frames: no picture waits for a later one
Focus fixed, at infinity a lens that refocuses changes its calibration
Transport RTSP over TCP wired, so nothing is lost; a frame never arrives broken
Who may publish the Pi itself only anyone may watch
Decoder, Orin GStreamer nvv4l2decoder NVIDIA's hardware; JetPack's own /usr/bin/gst-launch-1.0
Decoder, Mac GStreamer vtdec (VideoToolbox, Apple's hardware): the default, decoder:=auto ffmpeg holds back 2 frames (below)
Newest only yes a slow detector drops frames, never falls behind
Stamp arrival − delay (0.06 s) an estimate: measure it at the car

Why the decoder is a child program

NVIDIA's GStreamer plugins come with JetPack, not Pixi. The node runs JetPack's gst-launch-1.0 and reads raw pictures from its pipe (2.7 MB each, 41 MB/s: nothing for a pipe). So the node needs no GStreamer of its own, and the same code runs on a Mac with the gst environment's GStreamer, or ffmpeg. decoder:=opencv decodes inside the process instead, with an OpenCV built with GStreamer or FFmpeg.

Why its own environment

GStreamer's RTSP plugins aren't in the default environment, and adding them there means re-solving it (one re-solve on 23 Oct pulled Python 3.14). The gst environment has only GStreamer: adding it to pixi.lock changed no other environment's packages (all 22 environment-platform pairs compared, 24 Oct). The node runs its gst-launch-1.0 as the child program, as on the Orin.

/diagnostics says which decoder runs (decoder); stream-check checks a Mac gets gst-mac.

ffmpeg's 135 ms

Measured hop by hop: OpenH264 and x264 encode in 2 ms; MediaMTX adds nothing measurable; ffmpeg's decoder program holds back about 2 frames, with every low-delay flag tried (-flags low_delay, slice threads, one thread, -fflags nobuffer). GStreamer doesn't. So the Orin uses GStreamer, and so does a Mac now (decoder:=auto). OpenCV's FFmpeg reader was worse (over 1 s behind).

The time code: a row of black and white squares in the top left corner, carrying the time in ms, drawn before encoding and read after decoding (timecode.py). It survives OpenH264 at 2 Mbit/s (a test checks).
