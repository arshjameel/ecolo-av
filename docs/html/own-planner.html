Ben, 8 Nov: move off Nav2 one piece at a time, each beside Nav2 until it wins. The order: the costmap (done), then the path planner (this page), then the navigator. Since 14 Nov ours is the simulator's default (stack:=ours; Nav2's lattice with planner:=lattice or stack:=stock). Nav2 stays on the car.

The short version

  • Switch: planner:=own. Package aav_planner (Rust).

  • What it is: Nav2's State Lattice planner (our default) ported: the same primitives file, the same penalties from nav2.yaml, the Reeds-Shepp finish, the smoother. It checks the car's outline on the global costmap.

  • Drop-in: it answers Nav2's own ComputePathToPose action. The behaviour trees call it instead of planner_server; nothing else changes.

  • Test lot, 3 runs each: 12 of 12 legs, as Nav2's lattice; the same paths (within a metre), the same planning time (3-5 ms a call).

  • Campus: both planners failed the same legs (MPPI gave up, not the planner); ours took about 20 % longer to plan the long legs (180 ms against 150 ms). 15 Nov: 11 % off without changing a path (examples/campus.rs times the legs on the real map, no ROS; the outline's check no longer allocates). Splitting across cores doesn't pay: one step of the search is about 1 µs.

  • Not yet a winner: as good on the lot, a little slower on the campus.

  • Hybrid-A* too (14 Nov): planner_search:=hybrid runs Nav2's Hybrid-A* ported, on the same search (below). A challenger.

  • The lane-centre planner (16 Nov): path:=lane plans the right lane's centre along the lane map, the lattice only at the ends (below). A challenger.

How the swap works. navigation.launch.py remaps two names in bt_navigator: compute_path_to_pose and compute_path_through_poses → ours. The costmap step did the same with the clear service. Our navigator (page) calls both too.

What it does

start ─▶ A* over the moves ─▶ now and then: one curve straight to the goal? ─▶ smooth ─▶ path
          (primitives file)      (Reeds-Shepp; Dubins when forward only)
Step What it does Nav2's
moves 6 car-shaped moves from each of 16 headings (our file, tools/lattice_primitives.py); backwards too with reversing:=true LatticeMotionTable
does it fit? the car's padded outline at each pose along a move; most poses skip it (far from anything) GridCollisionChecker
cost of a move length × (1 + 2 × cost), × 1.05 for a turn, + 0.05 to change turn, × 2 backwards getTraversalCost
how far still? the larger of: round the obstacles (a grid search from the goal), and the car's shortest curve on open ground the two heuristics
the finish a Reeds-Shepp curve to the goal when one is clear (within 3 m), wider turns if cheaper AnalyticExpansion
smoothing pull each piece straighter, then re-join the true start and goal Smoother

Small changes from Nav2 (all in ros/drivers/aav_planner/PROVENANCE.md):

  • a node's pose changes only when a move makes it cheaper (Nav2 can mix two moves);
  • each move is checked for collisions on its own (Nav2 remembers the first move's answer for every later one). Safer, but more checks: part of why ours is slower;
  • the car's outline is checked where the path puts it (Nav2's is half a cell off).

A price on changing gear (cusp_penalty, 15 Nov, TODO 174)

Nav2 prices a backward move (reverse_penalty), never the stop and start between gears. In a narrow street that gave turns with 5 or 6 cusps, and the car drove them all (sim-check --three-point: 7 changes of direction). Two parts, ours only:

Part What
cusp_penalty (m) each change between forward and back costs that many metres of straight driving
The last curve with a price set, a Reeds-Shepp curve to the goal that changes gear is refused; the search, which prices each change, finds the end instead

Hybrid-A*: a second search (14 Nov)

Ben, 14 Nov: "Hybrid-A in our Rust planner: Now" (TODO 178). Nav2's SmacPlannerHybrid (Dolgov et al., 2010), ported into the same crate. A challenger:* the league's champion stays our lattice (lattice_rs); this is hybrid_rs.

  • Switch: planner:=own planner_search:=hybrid. Nothing else changes: the same node, actions, topics and costmap.

  • Settings: nav2.yaml's own Hybrid-A* block (GridBased), as Nav2's gets it: 72 headings, the planning radius (3.57 m), Reeds-Shepp with reversing:=true, else Dubins (forward only).

What is shared, what differs

Nav2 runs both planners on one A*; only the node type differs. Ours too:

                  ┌── the lattice: the primitives file's moves, each swept
search.rs (A*) ───┤
                  └── Hybrid-A*: 3 short arcs (6 reversing), checked where they end
  shared: the outline check, both estimates, the curve to the goal, the smoother
Part The lattice Hybrid-A*
moves from a heading 6 from our file, 16 headings straight, full left, full right; 72 headings (5°)
a move's length as the file draws it 0.63 m: just over a cell's diagonal, a 10° turn
backwards the same moves, driven backwards the same (Nav2's REVERSE, REV_LEFT, REV_RIGHT)
does it fit? along the whole move at its end only
a turn costs × 1.05, + 0.05 to change × 1.2 (no change penalty in nav2.yaml)
round the obstacles every cell 2 × 2 blocks (Nav2's default: a quarter of the work)

The lane-centre planner (path:=lane, 16 Nov)

TODO 148 and 169; the Fable review's change 7 ("follow the lane's centre, shift only to go round": Autoware's idea). A challenger beside the lanes layer (lane_costs:=true, own-costmap.md).

 car ─lattice─▶ ═══════ the right lane's centre, routed on the lane map ═══════ ─▶ goal
   (or straight, if the car is on its lane)            (straight, if the goal is on it)
Step What
The map lanes.yaml (the world's own; the campus's from OpenStreetMap), as the lanes layer reads it. Roads join where they share a point
The route Dijkstra over the road segments, never turning back on one; one-way roads only their way
The line each segment moved onto the right lane's centre (Road::lane, the same as the lanes layer), corners rounded at the turning radius (3.6 m)
The ends the lattice from the car to the line 8.1 m on, and from 8.1 m before the end to the goal. A car within 0.6 m and 20° of the line goes straight onto it 2 m on; a goal on its end, straight too
Off the map the start or goal over 20 m from every road (the lot), a route too short, the line over an obstacle, or no join: the lattice, all of it
Says how /drivers/nav/planner/status how: lane (and lane_m), or lattice: off_map, short, blocked, join_failed

Read with care.

  • Every leg missed, in every variant, was the computer, not the path. Another session ran a second simulator at the same time (load 17-37 on 16 cores): the local costmap went over 0.8 s old and the follower gave up, the sensor watchdog went stale, or (2 runs) the global costmap never came, so the planner had nothing to plan on. On the quietest runs all three reached every leg (path:=lane 6 of 6, the others 3 of 3).
  • The time is the same when a leg is reached: 43, 60 and 68 s.
  • The first version joined the car with the lattice at every replan (once a second); on the bends it backed up now and then: 10 reversals in 2 runs, the bend leg 86-109 s. Now a car on its lane is joined straight: 0 reversals in 6 runs.
  • The campus tour (2 runs each, the same load): 2, 1 and 4 of 6 legs; path:=lane fell back to the lattice there, as expected.

The verdict: a challenger, proposed for ours. It keeps the lane best (0.10 m off its centre, never over the centre line) and sways least, at the same time a leg. Not swapped yet: its reached column is lower on paper (the load), and the league asks for 3 clean runs of each on the same routes (TODO 148).
