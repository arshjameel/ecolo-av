Ben, 8 Nov: move off Nav2 to our own versions, one piece at a time, each beside Nav2 until it won. The order: the costmap, then a path planner in Rust, then the navigator. This page is the first piece. Since 14 Nov ours is the simulator's default (stack:=ours). Nav2 went on 17 Nov (as it was): ours is the only one, in the sim and on the car. The comparisons with Nav2's below are the record.

The short version

  • Switch: costmap:=own. Package aav_costmap (Rust).

  • What it is: Nav2's costmap layers ported: the map, the sensors' marks with ray clearing, the inflation round the car's outline. A local window and a global map.

  • Same input as Nav2: made from the same tuned nav2.yaml blocks.

  • Same output as Nav2: in the sim, stopped at the post, every one of 40,000 cells the same as Nav2's local costmap (5 runs of 5).

  • Lighter: our two costmaps 4-5 % of a core; Nav2's local one alone 7-9 %.

  • Scores: with ours, MPPI went round the box 3 of 3. The lidar-obstacle leg: 2 of 3 at first; 3 of 3 once the recovery cleared ours too (scores). Nav2's: 3 of 3 each.

  • Fast: 0.4 ms an update without ROS; about 0.5 ms in the sim.

  • Big maps: each costmap has its own thread, and the global one is worked out once per map, so the campus never makes the local one late (threads).

  • With tracker:=mppi_lag, MPPI reads ours instead of Nav2's.

  • From map chunks (16 Nov, on in stack:=ours): the global costmap holds only the 64 m chunks round the car and along the route, each inflated on its own core. Every leg as before, a third of the memory (chunks).

  • The local one too (16 Nov, on in stack:=ours): its map from the 3 x 3 chunks round the car, not /map. Every leg as before, half the node's memory; nothing of ours reads /map on the campus now.

What it does, layer by layer

Layer What it does Nav2's
static map /map's cells: 100 is lethal, unknown kept only in the global one StaticLayer
obstacles each scan point marks a lethal cell (within 12 m, 0.3-2 m high); each ray clears the cells it passes (to 15 m); the car's own cells are cleared ObstacleLayer
inflation cost falls away from each lethal cell: inscribed inside 0.25 m, then 252 × e^(-k(d - 0.25)) out to 2 m (local) or 3 m (global) InflationLayer
  • The rolling window moves in whole cells, so a mark stays on the same ground.
  • Scans are placed where the car was when the scan was taken (odometry kept 2 s).
  • One change from Nav2: the inflation finds the nearest obstacle exactly (a distance transform), not by Nav2's brushfire. The costs are the same.
  • Not ported: Nav2's 3D voxel layer, range sensors, filters. nav2.yaml uses none.
  • One addition (15 Nov): the radar's view clears. See below.
  • A challenger (15 Nov): the lanes layer (lane_costs:=true), off by default. See below.

The radar forgets what has gone (15 Nov, TODO 193)

The problem. A box in the lane: the radar marks it, the car stops. The box is taken away. The radar now returns nothing there, and in Nav2's rule only a ray to a point clears. So the box stayed in the radar's layer for ever. MPPI saw a wall that wasn't there and stood still until its progress check failed (10 s).

The fix. A point cloud source can say how wide its view is: view_angle (rad), on the radar's block in nav2.yaml, 120° (the TI radar's, and the simulator's stand-in).

Where the radar sees... Ours does
a point marks it, clears the ray up to it (as Nav2)
nothing, inside its view clears the ray out to raytrace_max_range (8.4 m), as a scan's inf does
outside its view (beside, behind) nothing: old marks stay
  • One ray every 0.5° (obstacle::view_points): 7 cm apart at 8.4 m.
  • The rays end past obstacle_max_range, so they never mark.
  • Only the radar's own layer: the lidar's and the cameras' marks are never touched.
  • Nav2's costmap had no such setting.
  • A real radar's sparse frames: a thing it misses in both frames of 0.2 s is cleared, and marked again at the next frame that sees it. the stop zone still reads the radar itself, not the costmap.

Threads and bounds (14 Nov)

The problem. On the campus map (6400 × 6400 cells, 41 million) every leg with costmap:=own tracker:=mppi_lag failed: "the costmap on .../local_raw: 0.84 s old (limit 0.80 s)". Both costmaps ran on one thread, and the global one rebuilt the whole map each second. While it did, the local one waited.

The fix, Nav2's way:

Nav2 does Ours now
each costmap has its own update thread (mapUpdateLoop) each costmap has its own thread; the ROS worker only gathers the inputs and hands them over
each update redoes only the bounds the layers touched (updateBounds, updateCosts) the global one is worked out once per map; after that only the cells the sensors touched, plus the inflation's reach
a late update loop is skipped and logged a busy thread skips to the newest inputs (skipped on the status) 
ROS worker: messages, timers ──▶ local thread:  update + publish (5 Hz, 0.4 ms)
                             └─▶ global thread: map once (0.5 s) + publish (1 Hz)

Before and after, without ROS (examples/timing.rs, campus size, this Mac with the simulator running beside it):

What Before After
global update, every second 477-539 ms 0.000 ms (nothing changed)
global update when the map comes the same 477 ms, once, on its own thread
global with a lidar layer on it the whole map again 0.25 ms (the scan's area)
making the two messages' cells 26 ms each publish only after a change
local waits for the global yes never
  • Still sent in full, once a second: the global grid (41 MB as 0-255, 41 MB as 0-100). Our planner checks its age (2 s limit), so it must keep coming. It goes out on the global thread, so the local one never waits for it. Not timed with ROS yet.
  • The same cells: a test checks an area update against a whole one, cell for cell.
  • The local one still rebuilds its whole 200 × 200 window: it moves every update, and that takes 0.4 ms.
  • New status values: publish_ms (the last publish) and skipped.

What's missing

  • The planner reads Nav2's global costmap (planner_server). Our planner (page, planner:=own) reads /drivers/nav/costmap/global_raw when costmap:=own is on too.
  • The global costmap's size: fixed 14 Nov (threads); with chunks (16 Nov) only 5 x 5 or so chunks (1.6 M cells). Sending it in full each second is left; partial updates (Nav2's _updates) only if that proves slow.
