Nav2's planner draws a path; a controller turns it into a speed and a turn, 20 times a second. We have two, and a script that compares them on the same drives in Gazebo.

The short version - MPPI is the default (since 22 Oct): it tries 2000 possible futures every cycle through a model of the car, and picks the best blend. It turns to a goal's heading; it costs more computer. - Regulated Pure Pursuit (RPP) is the other choice: it chases a point 2-4 m ahead on the path. Simple and light, but it aims only at a point, so it often arrives at a goal facing the wrong way. - Switch: controller:=rpp on sim.launch.py or navigation.launch.py. - Compare: pixi run controller-compare (about 25 min, headless, on this computer only; --legs 1 for a quick check). - Results, night of 22 Oct (Gazebo's lot, four runs): after five fixes (below), MPPI reached the first goal in 29-30 s in every run; Pure Pursuit looped there in two of three. In the best run both reached all 4 goals. MPPI uses twice the CPU (20% of a Mac core). - No more circling goals (29 Oct, TODO 17): Nav2 planned a new path every second, also in the last metres, where a new path is often a loop for a car. Now it keeps its path for the last 8 m (below).

What each one does

Regulated Pure Pursuit MPPI
Idea aim at a point ahead on the path; steer the arc that reaches it imagine 2000 futures 2.8 s long, score each, blend the best
Knows the car's shape no: only its turning radius, through the planner yes: the Ackermann turning radius and the outline (footprint)
An obstacle on the path slows, then stops before it may steer around it, if there's room
Settings to tune a few (lookahead, speeds) many (noise, temperature, eight scoring rules)
Computer light heavy: one of the reasons for a second Orin
Paper Macenski et al., 2023 Williams et al., 2017

Both read the same costmap and send the same message to the same velocity smoother, collision monitor, twist_mux and ego. Everything after the controller is the same.

Switch between them

pixi run sim world:=test_lot controller:=rpp       # Gazebo, Pure Pursuit following the path
ros2 launch aav_navigation navigation.launch.py controller:=rpp

controller:= takes mppi (the default) or rpp. For MPPI, the launch file copies the MPPI block of nav2.yaml under the name the behaviour trees ask for, FollowPath, and sets its turning radius, its reverse speed and its top speed from vehicle.yaml (aav_description/vehicle.py, nav2_tuned).

Compare them

pixi run controller-compare                    # both controllers, both tours
pixi run controller-compare --tours obstacle   # only the box in the lane
pixi run controller-compare --controllers mppi --speed 2
pixi run controller-compare --trackers lqr mpc --runs 3   # the trial trackers (docs/trials.md)

For each tour and controller the script starts Gazebo's test lot with no windows, on a ROS domain of its own (this computer only), sends the same goals, and records where the car really was (Gazebo's ground truth). Forward only by default; --reversing lets Nav2 back up, as it may on the car since 22 Oct. The results go to log/controller-compare/<time>/:

Circling a goal: found and fixed, 29 October (TODO 17)

Now and then the car drove a full loop at a goal it had nearly reached: about 50 m for a 21 m leg. Always the first leg (the left turn to 15, 15, facing north), any follower.

How it was found: pixi run controller-compare --tours lot --legs 1 --plans, 3 runs each of RPP, MPPI and aav_track's pure_pursuit and lqr. --plans keeps every plan, so each plan can be set against where the car really was when it came.

Why it looped Detail
A new path every second, from wherever the car is the tree replanned at 1 Hz, also 1-4 m from the goal
There, a new path is a loop the car runs a little behind on the last bend (heading 50-60°, the path 70-90°). To reach the goal's heading from there at the planner's 3.6 m radius, Smac's only path is a 21-25 m loop. An S-bend back onto the line needs up to 2 radii ahead (vehicle.final_approach): 7.2 m
Each new path resets the goal checker controller_server resets it with every path (setPlannerPath)
Not the cause the tolerances (1.0 m, 0.75 rad), the EKF's heading (worst 5-16° per leg), the trackers' own goal check (it already survives a new path)

The fix (navigate_to_pose_car*.xml): within 8 m of the goal the tree keeps the path it has, as long as it still ends at this goal, nothing blocks it (IsPathValid) and it is under 20 s old. Everywhere else it still replans every second. Stock Nav2 tree nodes only. The map window waits 25 s, not 3, before NO NEW PLAN there.

Making MPPI drive

With the settings first written for it (Nav2's examples for a small robot), MPPI in Gazebo sat still for 13 s, then crept at 0.2 m/s and drifted metres off a straight path. Found on 22 October, one cause at a time, by printing each layer's command twice a second:

What we saw Why Change
Its speed flipped between forward and back for 12 s the simulator let Nav2 reverse (reversing:=true by default), and MPPI dithered around zero the comparison runs forward only by default (--reversing to allow it)
Nav2 warned "Inconsistent configuration in collision checking" MPPI checked a circle; the costmaps use the car's 2 x 1 m outline CostCritic.consider_footprint: true
Fast samples all swerved it tried turn rates of ±0.4 rad/s, but the car turns at most 0.35 rad/s at 1 m/s (full lock): fast samples left the path, slow ones won wz_std 0.4 → 0.1
Speed built up by only 0.015 m/s each second its samples' speed changes were clipped by the 1 m/s² limit (0.05 m/s per step up, but 0.1 down), so its futures drifted slower ax_max 1.0 → 3.0 for its planning only (ego and the smoother still hold the car to 1 m/s²); ax_min stays at ego's -2.0
Still slow to commit small speed samples, a soft blend vx_std 0.2 → 0.4, temperature 0.3 → 0.15

After the changes: moving within 0.5 s, about 0.95 m/s, within about 0.5 m of a straight path. The settings and their reasons are in nav2.yaml, above the MPPI block.

Found on the way

  • The global EKF's heading jumps at walking pace. GPS gives only a position; the filter reads each fix's error (0.5 m in Gazebo) partly as a heading error, and the slower the car the bigger it reads it: ±20° at 0.2 m/s. Nav2 places its path with that pose, so the path swings round the car. Regulated Pure Pursuit, aiming 2-4 m ahead, barely notices; a slow MPPI did. It is probably also why a "straight" 10 m drive turned 38° on 21 October. Lowering the filter's heading noise helped but didn't cure it: a heading source (two GPS antennas, or the F9R's own fused heading) is the real fix. Open, for localization (proposal T6.3).
  • Late data: it was the messaging, not the Mac. Several runs were stopped by the sensor watchdog: the IMU, the wheels and both EKFs silent for 0.5-2 s at once, with the Mac 77% idle. Splitting Gazebo's adapter into one node per sensor (as on the car) didn't stop it. A bare publisher and subscriber beside Gazebo showed why: the publisher always on time, its messages up to 0.48 s late through Fast DDS, never over 0.05 s through Cyclone DDS. Cyclone is the default since 23 Oct (Your Mac).
  • The global EKF is up to 0.7 m off in Gazebo (0.5 m GPS noise), its heading up to 25°. In the slow stretches of the night's drives its heading stayed under 6°: the ±20° above was a car creeping for many seconds. The comparison now reports both per leg.
