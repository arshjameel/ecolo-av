A costmap is a grid laid on the ground. Every cell holds a cost:

  • free (0);
  • near something (inflated, 1–252);
  • inside something (lethal, 253–254);
  • unknown (255).

Nav2's planner searches the grid for a path the car can drive. Its controller follows that path and slows down near high-cost cells. nav2_costmap_2d builds the grids from layers. The settings are in ros/drivers/aav_navigation/config/nav2.yaml.

A 20 m window means 10 m ahead and behind, about 5 s of driving at 2 m/s, the most this car is meant to do this year. Make it larger only if the car gets faster.

  • Footprint: the car's outline, made from body in vehicle.yaml by the launch file. Nav2 checks that it doesn't enter lethal cells.
  • Inflation: at least the car's circumscribed radius, 1.93 m. This keeps paths away from walls rather than scraping them. The planner's is wider (22 Oct): with 2.0 m its path passed a building's corner 0.86 m away, and the collision monitor stopped the car there (Path followers).

Obstacles from the sensors

The local costmap has four sensor layers. Each is a stock Nav2 ObstacleLayer, switched on by navigation.launch.py.

Layer Reads On Marks Clears
obstacle_layer /sensors/radar/points (in radar_link) radar:=true: the bench always; the car where the machine file that runs navigation also runs radar; never Gazebo (its car has no radar) each point 0.3-8.4 m from the radar, -0.5 to 2 m high the cells between the radar and each point
camera_layer /perception/camera_obstacles, /perception/camera_view camera_obstacles:=true: the bench's argument, or the car's camera_obstacles part next to navigation. Off until the cameras are calibrated (Q22) where each detected object stands on the road, out to 12 m everything the camera sees, out to 12 m, before it marks
lidar_layer /sensors/lidar/scan (from lidar_scan, aav_lidar) where the lidar runs: on by default on the car (the Orin) and in Gazebo; lidar:=true on the bench points 0.3 m or more above the road (below), out to 12 m along each beam, out to 15 m
ghost_layer /safety/ghost/scan ghost:=true only (Ghost obstacles) the pretend person only old ghosts

Why the radar's numbers:

  • 8.4 m: the TI radar's range (aav_radar_ti cfg/base.cfg).
  • -0.5 to 2 m high: a radar sees no ground to reject, and its height is poor, so low points stay. Above 2 m is over the car (1.5 m tall).
  • Sparse: a few points per object. Each 5 Hz update uses the last 0.2 s: both radar frames, not one.

The camera layer is its own, so its clearing never wipes what the radar marked. Perception says how a box becomes a point on the road.

collision_monitor

Where the radar or the lidar runs, Nav2's command passes through collision_monitor on its way to twist_mux:

controller -> velocity_smoother -> /control/cmd_vel_smoothed -> collision_monitor -> /drivers/nav/cmd_vel -> twist_mux
Setting Value
Stop zone from the front bumper to 1 m ahead, 0.2 m wider than the body each side: made from vehicle.yaml by the launch file
Why 1 m at 1 m/s, ego stops the car in 0.25 m; the radar, mux, ego and brakes add about 0.4 m; the rest is for the radar's noise
Stops for 3 points in the zone, from the radar or the lidar (one stray point mustn't stop the car)
Also stops when a sensor it reads is silent for 1 s, or its TF is late
Reads the sensors' own points, not the costmap: it still works if the costmap goes wrong
  • It only guards Nav2. A person, a test script or the AI slot reach twist_mux without it.
  • After a stop it sends zeros for 2 s, then nothing; twist_mux then drops Nav2's slot and ego holds the car.
  • collision_monitor:=false leaves it out, for a test.

What the car does at an obstacle

On the bench (pixi run bench-check --obstacle, 11 October): a box as wide as the lane, 15 m ahead, Nav2 driving to a goal 30 m away. (A 1 m box first; but the car can weave a metre or more off its line, N7 below, and once it passed beside the box.)

Step What happened
The radar sees the box 8.4 m out; the costmap marks it
About 1 m before it both stop the car, within 0.15-0.7 s of each other: collision_monitor's stop zone (radar points) and Nav2's controller (the costmap). Either can be first
The car stands still 0.42 m before the box, from 1.02 m/s (0.53 m in a second run). With the lane-wide box on main: 0.44 m, and 0.26 m with the car starting 8° off the road
The box is taken away collision_monitor lets go at once; Nav2's recovery clears the costmap and the car drives on to the goal

Since 22 Oct, Nav2 steers round it

The table above is with plan_around:=false: the planner sees only the map, so the car stops and waits (the stop zone's tests run like that). By default the global costmap has the radar and lidar too, so the planner plans round the box where there's room: in Gazebo the car passed it 3.9 m clear and never stopped (pixi run sim-check --plan-around). Where the map leaves no room round it, the car stops as before. On the bench the map is empty, so the planner would leave the road: bench-check --obstacle runs with plan_around:=false.

Limits

  • What the radar stops seeing stays marked. A radar only clears along its points, so a box taken away stays in the costmap until Nav2's recovery clears it (it does, when the car is stuck).
  • The camera assumes a flat road, and ignores the lens's distortion. At 12 m, a camera tilted 1 degree more than vehicle.yaml says puts the point 2 m off.
  • A box cut off by the bottom of the picture is placed at the nearest road the camera sees (2.3 m ahead on the bench): the object is at least that close.
  • The numbers are guesses until the car is measured: the radar's mount (Q1-Q5) and the cameras' (Q22).

Body roll and the lidar (TODO 52e)

lidar_scan keeps points 0.3 m or more above the road, as if the body were level. In a turn the body leans out on its springs. On the outside of the turn the road then rises in the car's frame, by about distance out × tan(roll). Far enough out, the road looks like an obstacle.

   level:   lidar ●───────────────────────  road at 0 m: ignored
   rolled:  lidar ●─────────╲               the road 10 m out sits 0.23 m "up"
                              ╲___ road     (1.33°): still under 0.3 m

The highest road point the lidar sees within the costmap's 12 m (VLP-16 on the roof, 1.8 m up; aav_lidar ground.py):

Roll When Highest road point An obstacle?
0.23° 1 m/s, full lock (today) 0.04 m no
1.33° ego's limit: 2 m/s² sideways, with the springs' overshoot 0.23 m, 10 m out no
1.46° the limit 0.30 m the edge
1.95° 3 m/s on full lock, if ego didn't slow down 0.39 m, 11.5 m out yes

The verdict: safe up to 4 m/s. ego slows the car in turns to 2 m/s² sideways (max_lateral_accel), which holds the roll to 1.33° at most, even on a sudden full lock. At 5 m/s a sudden full lock still overshoots: the wheels turn faster than ego can brake (2.4° of roll). The springs are guesses: softer than about 7400 N/m a corner and the margin is gone. tools/tests/test_lidar_roll.py checks all of this together, and fails if someone raises max_lateral_accel or the 12 m, or lowers the 0.3 m.

Gazebo has no suspension, so it can't show this. The scan itself (out to 30 m) has road in it from about 0.6° of roll, but nothing marks the costmap past 12 m.

Don't level the cloud with the IMU's roll. imu_filter_madgwick finds "down" from the accelerometer, and in a turn the sideways push looks like tilt: 2 m/s² reads as 11.5° of roll, about 9 times the real 1.3°. Levelling would need that push taken out (speed × turn rate) first.

Map files

A Nav2 map is an image (PGM or PNG) plus a YAML file that says how to read it. ros/drivers/aav_navigation/maps/README.md covers the format line by line. In short:

  • one pixel is one cell;
  • white is free, black is blocked, grey is unknown;
  • resolution is metres per pixel;
  • origin is the map-frame position of the bottom-left pixel.

Don't paint maps by hand. Write a short spec and generate them:

tools/make_map.py ros/drivers/aav_navigation/maps/example_lot.spec.yaml

A spec lists:

  • the size in metres east and north;
  • the origin;
  • blocked rectangles and free rectangles.

tools/make_map.py --help (or the top of the file) shows the format. It needs only Python and PyYAML, not ROS.

Your first map, in ten minutes

  1. Open ros/drivers/aav_navigation/maps/example_lot.spec.yaml, and add a box under blocked::

    ```yaml
  - [15, -3, 18, 3]       # a box across the drive, to plan around
```

  2. Make the map again:

    ```bash
pixi run python tools/make_map.py ros/drivers/aav_navigation/maps/example_lot.spec.yaml
pixi run build
```

    It says wrote example_lot.pgm/.yaml: 320 x 240 px, 80 x 60 m. 3. Start Gazebo with the test lot: pixi run sim world:=test_lot. On a Mac, add gui:=false, then run pixi run gz-window and pixi run rviz in two more terminals. 4. In RViz, send a 2D Goal Pose at about (30, 0): 30 m straight ahead, past the box.

You should see the planned path bend around your box. In the test on 10 October it passed at y = 4.4 m, beside a box that ends at y = 3 m.

  1. Put the map back: git checkout ros/drivers/aav_navigation/maps/.

Two things this teaches

  • The box is only in the map, not in Gazebo's world. Nav2 avoids it because the map says so: that's how phase 3 works.
  • Leave room in front of an obstacle. The car turns no tighter than 2.86 m, and the costmap keeps 2 m of clearance, so a box 5 m ahead can't be swerved around. (The test tried it: Nav2 reported "collision ahead".)

Known problem, 10 October: the car doesn't finish this drive

In Gazebo the car follows the curve around the box, but overshoots the goal and ends about 6 m off. Nav2 then can't plan a way back, because the car only drives forward, so the goal fails. It's open work for the navigation team (N7, the lookahead). A straight 8 m goal on the empty map succeeds.

N7: why Nav2's turn command flips (11 October)

In Gazebo, on the empty map, each run on a fresh simulator. This was Regulated Pure Pursuit, before MPPI became the default (22 Oct; Path followers):

Turn-sign flips a second Straight goals (8 m, 25 m) The curve to (15, 8)
Lookahead 1.5 m (then) 0.86-0.99 0.48-0.71
Lookahead 3 m (tried) 0.85-1.44 0.25-0.52
  • Not the lookahead: a longer one didn't help on straight drives, so it stayed 1.5 m. Both reached every straight goal; each failed the curve once.
  • The GPS: the flips come in steps, five times a second, with the fixes. One step in ten turns the global EKF's heading (map -> odom) by more than 4 degrees, and the whole path swings with it.
  • Tried, not kept: with ekf_global trusting GPS less, one step in ten turned it by more than 0.6 degrees, and the flips fell to 0.06-0.5 a second; but one goal then overshot and looped for 45 s.

Where the map sits on the Earth

The map frame is pinned to the ground by the datum in ros/localization/aav_localization/config/navsat.yaml:

  • map (0, 0) is the datum's latitude and longitude;
  • x points east and y points north.

So a point 20 m east and 5 m north of the datum is (20, 5) on the map, in Nav2 goals and in RViz. The datum in the file now is a placeholder near Carleton.

To draw the real test site:

  1. Pick the datum: a corner of the lot, or the start spot. Survey it with the GPS (average a few minutes of fixes) and put its latitude and longitude in navsat.yaml.
  2. Measure the drivable area in metres east and north of it, with a tape, or by measuring on satellite imagery from the datum.
  3. Write the spec: the size, then the blocked rectangles (buildings, curbs, parked cars).
  4. Build the map and drive phase 3 in Gazebo first (world:=test_lot pairs with example_lot).

A costmap is not a road map

The global costmap only says which cells may be driven on. Roads, junctions and routing across a city are a separate kind of map: a road graph. Two tools handle that:

  • A street router (Valhalla, or OSRM). It turns a destination into a route of latitude/longitude points. Those points become a follow_gps_waypoints goal for Nav2. Our offline router, and the node that turns its routes into waypoints (street_router): Street routes.
  • Nav2's Route Server (nav2_route). It's released for Jazzy, in Nav2 1.3.13 as of October 2026, and follows a hand-made graph of lanes or paths. Consider it if the test site becomes a set of fixed lanes.

For this year's goals (an empty costmap, then a pre-rendered test site) a grid map plus GPS waypoints is enough.
