Ben, 7 Nov (0.3): "have the algorithms know about lag".

Nav2's MPPI plans as if the car obeys at once. It can't take our lag model without a C++ plugin, and we write no C++. So MPPI is ported to Rust, with the lag in its model. It began as a trial beside Nav2's MPPI; since 14 Nov it is the follower in stack:=ours, and since Nav2 went (17 Nov, as it was) the only MPPI. The comparisons with Nav2's MPPI below are the record.

The short version

  • Switch: tracker:=mppi_lag. Package aav_mppi_lag.

  • Reverses (14 Nov) as Nav2's MPPI does, with reversing:=true: it stops at each cusp, then backs up (below). The lot's turn back stalled at a cusp until the control cost was fixed (14 Nov, Cusps).

  • Same MPPI: Nav2's sampling, critics, softmax and smoothing, nav2.yaml's numbers.

  • New model: ego's ramps, the spine's speed loop, the pedals' and steering's lags.
  • Without ROS: it predicts the car 70 times better (8 mm off over 2.8 s, against 0.56 m).
  • On very slow actuators it keeps the path (0.1 m) where the lag-free MPPI loses it.
  • At vehicle.yaml's guesses both stay within a few cm: the lag is small there.
  • Fast: 1.7 ms a tick on an M4 Max (2000 samples x 56 steps); about 10 ms on the Orin.

  • Gazebo, 7 Nov: not a fair test: on a loaded computer the odometry stalled and it gave up (the log); while driving it was closer to the path (0.13 m against 0.20 m).

  • Our simulator, 8 Nov (newer; the numbers live on other pages): the lot tour, 3 runs, 11 of 12 legs (Nav2's MPPI 8: trials). In an all-ours stack it passes the campus's first goal at 2.5 m/s and uses twice Nav2's MPPI's CPU (33-39 % against 17-18 %: our navigator). Open: TODO 152 (slow earlier near the goal, fewer futures, time it on the Orin).

  • Costmap: our local costmap (page); Nav2's until 17 Nov.

  • No wiggle at speed (15 Nov, TODO 168): at 2.5 m/s the steering noise sized for 1 m/s made the steering jump side to side; it now shrinks with the speed (Oscillation).

  • Sways no more than Nav2 (15 Nov, the ours stack): the plan's staircase smoothed out, a steering control cost, the map's corrections taken slowly. Lot 0.021 m/s² (Nav2 0.018), campus 0.099 (Nav2 0.131), every leg (The sway that was left).

The model

Each sample is rolled out through this, step by step:

Stage What it does From
ego speed ramps at 1.0 up, 2.0 down m/s², either way; a change of direction passes through a stop; steering at 0.6 rad/s; holds the steering under 0.05 m/s ego.yaml, ackermann.py
speed loop the spine's PI along the gear: throttle from the speed error, brake past a coast band, a hold brake at a stop; the gear changes only while stopped (until then it brakes) spine-core's default (as Gazebo's SpeedLoop)
dead times each demand waits floor(t / dt + 0.5) steps vehicle.yaml (0 until measured)
throttle, brake first-order lag, exact: level += (u - level)(1 - e^(-dt/lag)) throttle_lag, brake_lag
speed max_accel x throttle (along the gear) - max_brake x brake - drag x v; the brake never reverses the car max_accel, max_brake, drag
steering deadband (on the motor's setpoint), motor at most steer_rate, linkage lag, + offset, full lock steer_*
pose bicycle with the new speed and steering, at the mid-step heading wheelbase

Where each sample starts: the pose and speed (odometry), ego's output, the measured steering, throttle and brake (while fresh), and the commands already sent that are still in their dead time. Nav2's starts from the pose and speed alone.

The trait: where the shared car model plugs in

The shared crate, car-model (ros/bringup/aav_description/car-model), is merged (0.3). LagModel follows its rules (the four rows above) and sits behind a trait (crates/mppi-lag/src/model.rs). The shared crate implements the trait behind the cargo feature shared (src/shared.rs, tested against LagModel); the node still runs LagModel (TODO 139).

pub trait CarModel: Sync {
    type State: Clone + Send + Sync + Debug;
    fn at_rest(&self, pose: Pose, speed: f64) -> Self::State;
    fn step(&self, state: &mut Self::State, command: Command, dt: f64);
    fn observe(&self, state: &mut Self::State, seen: &Observation);
    fn pose(&self, state: &Self::State) -> Pose;
    fn speed(&self, state: &Self::State) -> f64;
    fn steering(&self, state: &Self::State) -> f64;
    fn wheelbase(&self) -> f64;
    fn lag_aware(&self) -> bool;
}
Model What Use
LagModel the spec above (the car behind ego) model: lag, the default
IdealModel obeys at once (Nav2's assumption) model: ideal: the same MPPI without the lag, for comparison
Driven<M> ego's ramps in front of any model always

What differs from Nav2's MPPI

Nav2 Ours Why
Samples (vx, wz), each step clipped to ax, az (speed, steering), clipped to the range only ego's ramps are in the model; Nav2's clipping drifts samples slower
Noise white correlated, AR(1) 0.8 see below
Steering noise at speed wz_std: the angle's shrinks as 1 / speed 0.15 rad up to 1 m/s, then x (1 / speed)² the same sideways spread at every speed (Oscillation)
Control cost gamma / std² x Σ u e gamma x u' Σ⁻¹ e, Σ the AR(1) noise's covariance; Nav2's for white noise Nav2's form on correlated noise stopped the car (Cusps)
Sent element 1 element 0 the dead times already hold what was sent
Critics the eight in nav2.yaml the same eight, same weights; PathAngle's three modes; and our LaneCritic with a lane map (below) Nav2 has no critic for a lane
At a cusp (reversing) the critics' goal is the path's end the cusp, while one lies ahead; the goal check waits for the last stretch the car stops there as at a goal; Nav2 stops only because the path ends
Cusp reached within the inversion tolerances the same, or stopped within the goal's tolerances; or stopped 3 s within 2 m (cusp_reach) it may stop just outside them, or be held there by the stop zone (Cusps)

Why the noise is correlated

Through a lagging steering, white noise averages away: every sample drives alike, and MPPI learns nothing. Without ROS, on a slow car (steering 0.5 rad/s, 0.3 s lag, 0.2 s dead time), an S-curve (2 x 16 m) at 1 m/s, mean distance off the path (measured with the first, forward-Euler version of the model):

Noise Lag-aware MPPI Lag-free MPPI
white (Nav2's) lost the path (5.6 m off) 0.033 m
correlated 0.8 0.036 m 0.018 m

So ours samples smooth wiggles (colored-noise MPPI). noise_correlation: 0.0 gives Nav2's. The control cost must then use the same noise's covariance (Cusps).

Cusps

Found 14 Nov: measure controllers --stacks nav2 own --tours lot --reversing. On the "turn back" leg (35, 42) facing east to (20, 42) facing west, Nav2's MPPI got there in 59 s; mppi_lag gave up 4 times ("no progress in time"), 22.6 m driven.

Our planner's path there: back round a 3.6 m bend, a cusp, then forward round to face west. Rebuilt without ROS (turn_back in tests/closed_loop.rs):

Step What happened
1 backing up, MPPI steered only 0.1 rad of the 0.4 needed
2 0.7 m outside the bend, it stopped 1.9 m beside the cusp
3 nothing moved it on: Stuck after 10 s, every time

Cause: the control cost. MPPI adds gamma x u x noise / std² for each sample (Nav2's form, made for white noise). Ours samples correlated noise, and kept that form:

Noise Spread of the control cost
white (Nav2) 1 x
correlated 0.8, white form (ours before) 3 x: it outweighed the critics and pulled speed and steering to 0
correlated 0.8, its own covariance (ours now) 1/3 x

Fix 1: the cost uses the noise's own covariance (control_cost in optimizer.rs). With noise_correlation: 0 it is Nav2's exactly.

Fix 2: starting rolling and 0.5 m off the path, the car stopped 0.6 m from the cusp: outside the inversion tolerance (0.5 m), so still Stuck. Now a cusp also counts once the car has stopped within the goal's tolerances (1 m, 0.75 rad), as the path would end at its goal. The progress check starts again at each new stretch.

Closed loop, the turn back Before After
from a stop on the path Stuck at 18.9 s reached in 25.9 s
rolling at 0.5 m/s, 0.5 m and 11° off Stuck at 18.3 s reached in 27.6 s (needs fix 2)

The planner's own path (exported from lattice-planner on the lot's map), 8 seeds each: 0 of 8 before, 8 of 8 after; also with a slow car, rolling at 0.8 m/s, or started 0.2 rad off. In the simulator: not yet run with the fix.

Oscillation: the cause

In short (15 Nov, TODO 168): the steering noise, sized for 1 m/s, spread the futures six times as wide at 2.5 m/s, so the steering jumped side to side. The fix: above 1 m/s the noise shrinks with the speed squared (steer_std_speed), never under 0.024 rad (steer_std_min). Moved to the mppi-lag log on 18 Nov (a dated record).

Follow in odom, correct slowly

TODO 186. The GPS moves the map; the follower should not feel it.

The problem

The follower drives in odom, on the smooth wheels and IMU. The path comes in map. The global EKF's map -> odom jumps with each GPS fix: in the sim about 0.09 m sideways, 5 times a second. Moved by the newest map -> odom, each new path lands up to 9 cm beside the car. The follower sees a cross-track error and steers at it.

The fix

  newest map -> odom ──(jumps 0.09 m at 5 Hz)──┐
                                               ▼
  held map -> odom ◄── at most 0.1 m/s and 1°/s, measured at the car
       │               taken at once past 2 m or 10° (a relocalisation, logged)
       ▼
  path (map) ──► path (odom) ──► MPPI; the running path moves as the held one moves
Part What
The held map -> odom the follower's own; each new path comes into odom by it
The slew toward the newest, at most map_correction_speed (0.1 m/s) and map_correction_turn_rate (1°/s)
Measured at the car a 1° turn about odom's origin 100 m away is 1.7 m there, but 0 at the car
The snap past map_correction_snap_distance (2 m) or _snap_angle (10°): taken at once
Off map_correction:=newest (the launch's default): each new path by the newest. On: map_correction:=slow
Status the sim's ours stack takes it (15 Nov, with the two sway fixes); navigation.launch.py and the car keep newest. The obstacle check's stall (TODO 193) happens with newest too
Watch it map_gap_m on /drivers/nav/track/status: the held one's gap at the car

Why 0.1 m/s: odometry drifts 1-2 % of the distance (0.05 m/s at 2.5 m/s), so the held one keeps up with real drift, but a 9 cm jump takes about a second to come in. A complementary filter (Higgins 1975): the fast part of the pose from odometry, the slow part from the map. aav_track runs the same rule (slow_frame.py).

lag_aware: so ego doesn't cancel the lag twice

Ego's lead (0.3) cancels lag for drivers that don't know about it. It must turn off while a lag-aware driver drives.

Ego's contract: /drivers/<slot>/lag_aware, std_msgs/Bool, reliable, transient local, depth 1. This drives through navigation's slot: /drivers/nav/lag_aware.

When Says
start, exit false
a path starts (model: lag) true
the path ends false
model: ideal always false
