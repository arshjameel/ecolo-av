Navigation turns a goal into driving: plan a path, then steer along it. On our car it is just one more driver: it proposes a speed and a turn rate, like the joystick does.

Our stack (stack:=ours) does it, every part in Rust. Each part is a port of a part of Nav2, ROS's stock navigation, which drove the simulated car from 10 Oct to 17 Nov (Nav2, as it was). The ideas below are Nav2's; the code is ours.

You'll learn:

  • the parts: goal, navigator, planner, path follower, costmaps, smoother, stop zone;
  • how a lattice planner finds paths a car can really drive;
  • how pure pursuit steers, with one example in numbers;
  • what happens when things go wrong: recoveries and the stop zone;
  • how GPS waypoints become goals;
  • our settings, and where they live.

Costmaps have their own page: Costmaps. The big picture of maps and GPS is in Maps and position.

        (replan,     (lattice)   (mppi_lag)                  (radar,        ─► twist_mux ─► ego
         recover)        └── costmaps ──┘                     lidar)
Part Job Ours (package)

Navigation never talks to the wheels. ego turns its turn rate into a steering angle (Learn: control).

1. The planner: a lattice of car moves

A* searches a grid for the cheapest way from A to B. A plain grid search gives paths with sharp corners: fine for a robot that spins on the spot, not for a car.

A lattice planner searches in (x, y, heading), and only joins moves a car can make: straight on, and arcs no tighter than the car's turning radius (2.87 m, from vehicle.yaml). The moves are made once, ahead of time, into a file (config/lattice_ackermann*.json). So every path it returns is one our car can follow.

Hybrid-A* (planner_search:=hybrid) is the same idea, with the arcs worked out as it searches.

Setting (nav2.yaml's lattice block) Ours Means
allow_reverse_expansion true with reversing:=true forward and back (Reversing)
reverse_penalty 2.0 a reverse metre costs twice a forward one
cusp_penalty 4 m each change of gear costs as much as 4 m of driving (own planner)
headings 16 (32 with lattice_headings:=32) the directions a path may point in

2. The path follower

Our follower is MPPI: it tries 2000 futures through a model of the car, and picks a blend of the best (MPPI with lag). Pure pursuit is the easiest way in, and tracker:=pure_pursuit runs it (Trials).

Pure pursuit is how you steer a bike: look at a point on the path a little ahead (the carrot), and turn so you'd arc through it.

            carrot ●   (on the path, the lookahead distance ahead)
                  ╱
                 ╱  arc through it
           car ▲

The arc's curvature = 2 × (how far the carrot is to the side) ÷ (lookahead)².

Example: the carrot is 2.0 m ahead and 0.4 m to the left.

Step Sum Result
Curvature 2 × 0.4 ÷ 2.0² 0.2 per m (a 5 m circle)
Turn rate at 1.0 m/s 1.0 × 0.2 0.2 rad/s → /drivers/nav/cmd_vel
ego's steering angle atan(1.57 × 0.2 ÷ 1.0) 0.304 rad

Done when within 1.0 m and 0.75 rad (43°) of the goal: no tighter than GPS without RTK can tell (22 Oct; tighter, the car looped to fix misses it couldn't see). Stuck if in 10 s it neither moves 0.5 m nor turns 0.5 rad (29°): rocking back and forth in a three-point turn still counts.

4. The navigator: the boss

The navigator is one state machine (our navigator). It does what Nav2's behaviour trees did for a car:

goal ─► plan ─► follow ─► plan again, at most every second
          │        │        (not in the last 8 m: keep the path, unless blocked or 20 s old)
          ▼        ▼
   clear global  clear local      ◀── a failure a recovery can fix
          └────────┴─► in turn: clear both ─► wait 5 s ─► back up 1 m (reversing only)
                       up to 6 times, then give up
Made for a car Why
No spin a car can't turn on the spot
Back up only with reversing 1 m at 0.25 m/s, after the wait; only when something sees behind the car
Keep the path for the last 8 m a path planned a few metres from the goal is often a loop for a car: the car circled goals (TODO 17)

Reversing

One switch, reversing:=. On everywhere: Ravensim, the bench, and the car since 1 Nov (Ben: "reverse and uturn should be in"). The wheel sensors can't tell direction yet (H5), so the speed's sign is the last command's: wrong only if the car is pushed or rolls back (tests, 4.25). On the car it works only where something sees behind it: the roof lidar (the radar faces forward; the rear camera, later). Without one, car.launch.py keeps navigation forward only and says why (vehicle.blind_behind).

reversing:=false reversing:=true (the default everywhere)
Planner forward only forward and back; a reverse metre costs 2x, a change of gear 4 m
MPPI's vx_min 0 -0.5 m/s
Smoother's lowest speed 0 -0.5 m/s
Navigator's recoveries no back-up back up 1 m at 0.25 m/s
Stop zone the box ahead the box ahead going forward, a box behind reversing (5)

The 0.5 m/s is max_reverse_speed in vehicle.yaml: the one reverse speed for the whole car. ego and the spine (MAX_REVERSE_SPEED in spine-core, also for the RC transmitter) cap reversing at the same number, whoever drives.

Where it is set How
vehicle.yaml max_reverse_speed: 0.50
aav_description/vehicle.py nav2_rewrites(...) and nav2_tuned(...) write it into a copy of nav2.yaml, the settings our nodes read
car.launch.py, sim.launch.py, bench.launch.py reversing:=true by default in each; on the car it needs the lidar running, or it stays forward only
Ravensim check pixi run sim-check --rear-obstacle: backing up, the car stops for a post behind it
Tests test_vehicle.py (the values, and that each rewrite lands on a real key)

People can always reverse (joystick, keyboard, RC): this switch is navigation's only.

5. The stop zone: the last word

aav_stop_zone sits between the smoother and twist_mux, and reads the radar's and the lidar's own points, not the costmap. So it still stops the car if the planner, the follower or the costmap goes wrong. It is a port of Nav2's collision_monitor, which it replaced on 17 Nov after a shadow run (the swap).

Setting Ours
Stop zone going forward (or standing): a box from the front bumper to 1 m ahead. Reversing: a box from the rear bumper to 1 m behind. Each 0.2 m wider than the car each side. The command's speed picks the box
Behind the car only the lidar sees there (all round), not the radar. The rear camera's obstacles: later (TODO 25)
Points needed 3 (one stray point mustn't stop the car)
Radar or lidar silent for 1.0 s → stop
Guards only navigation. The joystick and keyboard reach twist_mux without it

It runs with the radar or the lidar: on the bench, on the car, and in Ravensim.

When it stops the car backing up, the follower may pull forward and try again; each try stops at the box. Nothing is hit, but the car rocks until the way is clear.

6. GPS waypoints

street route (lat/lon points) ─► follow_gps_waypoints ─► each to map x, y (the datum) ─► one goal after another
  • street_router and the language tools send the route; the navigator serves /follow_gps_waypoints (nav2_msgs/action/FollowGPSWaypoints: Nav2's name and message, so no sender changed).
  • It turns each point into map x, y from the datum (Learn: GPS).
  • The planner doesn't know where the road is: it plans across its costmap between points. Staying in the lane is the lane planner's (path:=lane) and the lane keeper's job.
