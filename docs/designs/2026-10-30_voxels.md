# Voxels: the lidar as coarse cubes, read once, shared by Nav2

* **Author:** Ben (the ask), written up 30 Oct 2026
* **Status:** Tentative: revised after the EGM review (design, codebase), 30 Oct; built as a trial beside the flat scan (`voxels:=true`); the Gazebo runs in `docs/trials.md`

Ben, 30 Oct: "reducing the navigation area down to cubes. Less precise but this is a car
not a needle. We don't need fine fine detail. we need to quickly know something is in our
way."

## The idea in one line

One node reads `/sensors/lidar/points` once, keeps only what stands **between the road and
the roof plus a margin**, and publishes it as **0.25 m cubes**. The local costmap and
collision_monitor read the cubes instead of the flat scan. NDT stays on the raw cloud.

```
/sensors/lidar/points ──► voxels ──► /perception/voxels/cubes ──► local costmap (mark)
   (0.9 MB, 10 Hz)          │                                 └─► collision_monitor
                            ├──────► /perception/voxels/ground ─► local costmap (clear)
                            └──────► /perception/voxels/status   (ms, cubes, tilt)
```

## What the flat scan gets wrong today

`lidar_scan` (aav_lidar) keeps, for each of 720 directions, the **nearest** point between
0.3 m and 2.0 m above the road, measuring height from a level car.

| Problem | Today | With cubes |
|---|---|---|
| A leaning car (a turn, braking) | the road rises in `base_link`: past **1.46° of roll** the road within 12 m counts as an obstacle (`aav_lidar ground.py` `max_safe_roll`; `test_ground.py`) | the road's plane is fitted to each cloud; heights are measured from it |
| What stands behind the nearest thing | lost: one range a direction | kept: every cube |
| What is under an overhang | the overhang hides it (nearest only) | both are cubes |
| Size | 3 kB | ~10 kB of cubes, ~20 kB of road |

## Stock first: three ways to get cubes into the costmap

| Option | What it is | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Nav2 **VoxelLayer** (`nav2_costmap_2d`, in our env, Apache-2.0) | a 3D grid in the costmap, 3D raytracing from the sensor | **Later, a second trial.** It could read our cubes and road with `sensor_frame: lidar_link`, and its 3D rays would keep a low box near the car marked (see Clearing). Not first: the cubes would have to be sent in `lidar_link`, 3D raytracing costs more, and the 2D layer is what the scan uses today, so the comparison stays one change at a time |
| **Spatio-Temporal Voxel Layer** (STVL 2.5.5, RoboStack Jazzy) | OpenVDB voxels; clears by the sensor's view (frustum) and by time | **No.** RoboStack has it only for `linux-64` and `linux-aarch64`, **not `osx-arm64`** (`pixi search`, 30 Oct): the team's Macs can't run it. (LGPL-2.1, loaded as a plugin: fine, but moot) |
| **Our node** + stock **ObstacleLayer** | cubes made once; the costmap's plain 2D layer reads them as `PointCloud2` | **Yes.** One reader of the cloud; the layer and collision_monitor are stock; every reader shares the same cubes |

Ben's ask, "a topic so several readers share them", is the third.

## The cubes

| Choice | Picked | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cube edge | **0.25 m** | measured on a made-up lot (below): 788 cubes at 0.25 m, 372 at 0.5 m, 982 at 0.2 m, about the same time. 0.5 m would blur a 1 m stop zone by half a cube |
| A cube's point | the cube's **nearest point to `base_link`** (one per cube) | a cube's centre can be 0.18 m (the diagonal) from the real face: too much for a 1 m stop zone, and `footprint_padding` is only 0.05 m. The nearest point keeps the face where it is, at the same size |
| Grid | anchored on `base_link`, rebuilt every cloud | no TF, no memory: the costmap does the remembering |
| Height kept | **0.3 m** to body + **0.5 m** (2.0 m) above the **fitted** road (`vehicle.obstacle_heights`) | the scan's numbers. Points 0.15-0.3 m up are neither road nor obstacle (a kerb): as today |
| Range | 30 m flat from `base_link` | the costmap marks to 12 m |
| Noise | every cube with 1 point or more | collision_monitor's `min_points: 3` ignores a stray (and see the risk below) |
| Message | `sensor_msgs/PointCloud2`, `x, y, z` float32 | ObstacleLayer, collision_monitor, Foxglove and RViz all read it |

Each cube marks one 0.1 m costmap cell: a wall is a dotted line of cells about 0.15 m
apart. Inflation (2 m) fills it; the stop zone counts points, not cells.

## Ground on a tilting car

Each cloud: take the **lowest point of each 2 m square** between 2 and 20 m (the road, or a
wall's foot where no road shows). Fit a plane `z = a·x + b·y + c` through the lower three
quarters, then through those within 0.3 m of it, then within 0.1 m: walls and cars drop out.

| Check | If it fails |
|---|---|
| 20 or more squares agree | the road is taken as **flat** (z = 0), as the scan does |
| tilt at most **4°** (a body leans about 3° on its springs) | flat |
| the road within 0.2 m of `base_link` | flat |
| its squares within **0.05 m** (RMS) of the plane | flat: a crest or a crowned road is not one plane |

| Risk (design review) | How big |
|---|---|
| A wrong fit lifts the road and hides something low | at most 4° wrong: 0.56 m at 8 m, so a 1 m box there still shows (`test_a_box_among_parked_cars_still_shows`) |
| A crowned road | the fit can only come out too low (more cubes), not too high (`test_a_crowned_road_...`) |
| Later, if needed | a ground height per square instead of one plane |

The status says `fitted` and the roll and pitch. Gazebo has no suspension: there the fit
should come out flat. The made-up lot tests lean it 2-3°.

Why not the IMU? It measures against gravity, not the road: a lot that slopes 2° tilts it.

## The car itself

Points inside the body's outline, at any height, are dropped: **0.3 m** behind and beside
the body (rear camera, mirrors), **0.1 m** ahead of the bumper (the front cameras at
x = 1.75 m, z = 1.55 m stick out of Gazebo's body box). The scan's box, now one function
both use: `aav_lidar scan.body_box()`.

## Clearing: cubes that go away

ObstacleLayer clears first, by 2D rays from the cloud's origin to each point, then marks
(`obstacle_layer.cpp` updateBounds; checked in the review).

| Source | Topic | marking | clearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| `voxels` | `/perception/voxels/cubes` | yes | yes |
| `voxels_ground` | `/perception/voxels/ground` (flat 0.25 m squares where the road was seen) | no | yes |

A ray to the road behind a spot that is now empty clears it, as the scan's `inf_is_valid`
did. Marks come from the newest cloud only (`observation_persistence: 0`).

Both sources set every height and range themselves: Nav2's per-source defaults
(`min/max_obstacle_height` 0, `raytrace_max_range` 3 m) would drop the road or cut the
rays short. The layer's own heights bound every source, so `voxels:=true` widens them to
-1.5 to 4.0 m (in `odom`; the real height cut is the node's).

| Known gap (as today's scan) | |
|---|---|
| The roof can't see the road nearer than ~6.7 m, nor a low box close in front | rays to the road beyond pass over it in 2D and **clear** it. Only the radar's own layer keeps such a box. VoxelLayer (3D rays) would not clear it: the second trial |

## collision_monitor

Its `voxels` source: `type: pointcloud` on the cubes, `min_height` -1.5, `max_height` 4.0
(its defaults, 0.05-0.5 m in `base_link`, would drop every cube). The node publishes every
cloud, empty or not, so `source_timeout` (1 s) stops Nav2 only if the node goes quiet.

**Risk:** cubes count fewer points than the scan's rays. From the roof, a thing in the
stop zone shows only above about 1.3 m: a thin pole may give fewer than `min_points: 3`.
The post test below measures it; a thin pole is a TODO.

## Frames, time and rate

| | Rule |
|---|---|
| Frame | `base_link` (the cloud turned by `vehicle.yaml`'s `lidar_link`, as `lidar_scan` does) |
| Stamp | the cloud's own |
| Rate | every cloud (10 Hz); in: reliable, keep last 1 (as `lidar_scan` and `ndt`: the Gazebo bridge and the velodyne driver publish reliable) |
| Out | reliable, depth 1 (the costmap and collision_monitor subscribe best effort: they still connect) |

## The node: Python, by measurement

| | |
|---|---|
| Package | `ros/perception/aav_voxels` (ament_python): `cubes.py` (no ROS, tested), `voxels_node.py` |
| Cost | **4-6 ms a cloud** for the cubes on a made-up 28,800-ray lot (numpy, Ben's Mac); the flat scan 0.2 ms |
| Rust? | not at that cost (5 % of the 100 ms between clouds). Rust (as `aav_ndt`) if the Orin measures over 20 ms |

`/perception/voxels/status` (`DiagnosticStatus`, every cloud): `ms`, `cubes`,
`ground_cubes`, `points`, `fitted`, `roll_deg`, `pitch_deg`, `clouds_dropped`.

## The switch

`voxels:=true` (needs `lidar:=true`) in `navigation.launch.py`; `sim.launch.py` and
`car.launch.py` pass it on. Default **false**: nothing changes.

| With `voxels:=true` | |
|---|---|
| starts | `voxels` |
| local costmap's `lidar_layer` | sources `voxels voxels_ground` instead of `lidar` (in `vehicle.nav2_tuned`) |
| collision_monitor | its `voxels` source in the lidar's place |
| `lidar_scan` | only with `amcl:=true` (AMCL reads the scan); `/sensors/lidar/scan` is then not live, so sim-check ignores it |
| the planner's costmap (`plan_around`) | the same `lidar_layer` |

The debug HUD keeps its own cubes from the raw cloud: it draws the road too (it could
read a `base_link` cloud: its frame lookup gives identity there).
